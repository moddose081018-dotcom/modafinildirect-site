What Is Modafinil?

Modafinil is a wakefulness-promoting agent — a prescription-only medicine licensed in the UK by the Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA) to treat excessive sleepiness associated with narcolepsy. Doctors have also prescribed it off-label for daytime sleepiness caused by obstructive sleep apnoea and shift work sleep disorder, particularly for people working night shifts. It is sold under the brand name Provigil and as generic modafinil tablets.

Outside the clinic, modafinil has a reputation as a "smart drug" or smart pill. Research from the University of Oxford and Harvard reviewed the clinical evidence and found modafinil can improve alertness, attention and some aspects of executive function in healthy, sleep-deprived adults. That's why it's popular among students, shift workers and professionals. It is not a supplement and it is not an amphetamine — it acts on wakefulness pathways in the brain rather than flooding it with stimulant chemicals, which is why it carries a lower risk of dependence than traditional stimulants.

Modafinil Dosage

The standard clinical dose of modafinil is 200mg once daily, taken in the morning. Many first-time users find that half a tablet — the equivalent of 100mg tablets — is enough, with fewer side effects. Start low, take it early in the day to protect your sleep, and don't exceed 200mg in 24 hours without medical advice. Effects typically last 8–12 hours. Side Effects and Warnings

Modafinil is well tolerated by most users, but it is a prescription medicine for a reason. The most common possible side effects are headache, nausea, dry mouth, reduced appetite, anxiety and trouble sleeping if taken late in the day. These are usually mild and tend to fade with regular use.

Serious side effects are rare but real. Stop taking modafinil and seek immediate medical attention if you develop skin rashes or blistering (a sign of Stevens-Johnson syndrome), chest pain, difficulty breathing, swelling of the face or throat, or severe changes in mood or depression. Long-term dependence is unusual but misuse at high doses increases the risk.

Modafinil is not suitable for every patient. Inform your doctor or GP before use if you are pregnant or breastfeeding, have a history of heart problems, liver problems, anxiety or depression, or take other medication — modafinil can reduce the effectiveness of hormonal contraception. A short consultation with a healthcare professional is worth having if anything in your medical history gives you pause. Full details are on our modafinil side effects page.
