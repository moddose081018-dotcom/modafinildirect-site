What Is Provigil (Modafinil)?

Provigil is the original brand name for modafinil, developed by Cephalon (now a division of Teva Pharmaceuticals). It was first approved in the UK and US in the late 1990s for narcolepsy and later expanded to other sleep disorders. The Provigil brand name is what most people recognise — but the patent expired years ago, which opened the market to generics.

Provigil contains modafinil 200mg per tablet. That's the same dose, the same molecule, as every generic modafinil product. Modafinil is a wakefulness-promoting agent: it acts on dopamine and other pathways in the brain to keep you alert throughout the day without the crash of a classic stimulant.

What Is Modafinil Used For?

In the UK, modafinil is a prescription-only medicine licensed by the MHRA to treat excessive sleepiness associated with narcolepsy. Doctors prescribe it off-label for other causes of excessive daytime sleepiness, including obstructive sleep apnoea and shift work sleep disorder. Beyond that, it's widely used as a cognitive enhancer by people who want to stay alert and focused on long days — clinical reviews show modest improvements in attention and alertness, particularly in the sleep-deprived.

Provigil Dosage

The standard dose of Provigil, and of generic modafinil, is one 200mg oral tablet in the morning. Many people find that half a tablet — the equivalent of 100mg tablets — is enough. Start there if you're new to it, take it early in the day, and don't exceed 200mg in 24 hours without medical advice. Effects last 8–12 hours.

Side Effects and Warnings

Provigil and generic modafinil share the same possible side effects because they're the same medicine. The common ones are headache, nausea, dry mouth, reduced appetite and trouble sleeping if taken late. Most are mild and reduce with regular use. Serious side effects — a severe skin rash, chest pain, difficulty breathing or swelling of the face or throat — are rare, but if they occur stop taking modafinil and seek immediate medical attention.

Modafinil is not suitable for every patient. Inform your doctor or a healthcare professional before use if you have a cardiovascular, liver or kidney condition, a history of anxiety or depression, or any relevant medical history. It reduces the effectiveness of hormonal contraceptives and other birth control, so use an extra method. Avoid alcohol, which increases side effects. Don't take it if you're pregnant or breastfeeding. The risk of dependence is low, but misuse at high doses raises the chance of an adverse reaction. Full details are on our side effects page.

The price gap comes down to patent recovery. Cephalon spent decades and hundreds of millions developing Provigil, running clinical trials, and getting it approved. They recovered those costs through exclusive pricing while the patent held.
