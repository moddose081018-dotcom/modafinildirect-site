Why Generic Modafinil Costs Less Than Provigil

Provigil is the original brand-name version of modafinil, manufactured by Cephalon (now owned by Teva). A private prescription for Provigil in the UK typically runs to £200–£400 for 30 tablets — around £7–13 per tablet. On the NHS, modafinil is only prescribed for narcolepsy, so most people can't get it that way at all.

Generic modafinil tablets contain the identical active drug at the same 200mg dose. The difference is the manufacturer. Companies like Sun Pharma and HAB Pharma produce generics under licence once the original patent expires. The drug itself is the same molecule, so modafinil 200mg from Sun Pharma does the same job as brand name Provigil.

The cost difference isn't about quality — it's about development costs. Cephalon spent decades and hundreds of millions developing and getting Provigil approved. Generics manufacture an already-proven compound without those R&D overheads.

What Is Modafinil and Why Is It a Prescription Medication?

Modafinil is a wakefulness-promoting agent — technically a weak dopamine reuptake inhibitor rather than a classic stimulant — that acts on the brain to promote wakefulness and alertness. It's a prescription-only medicine in the UK, licensed by the MHRA to treat excessive sleepiness associated with narcolepsy. Narcolepsy is a chronic condition that causes overwhelming daytime sleepiness, and modafinil is the first-line treatment for it. Doctors also prescribe it off-label for other sleep disorders — obstructive sleep apnoea, shift work sleep disorder and idiopathic hypersomnia — and it's widely used to manage daytime sleepiness and enhance cognitive performance on long working days.

It's a prescription medication because it's a medicine with possible side effects and interactions, not because it's dangerous or addictive — the risk of dependence is low, which is why it isn't a controlled drug in the UK. Buying and possessing it without a valid prescription is not an offence; see our UK legality page for details.

Is Modafinil Still Prescribed in the UK, and Do I Need a Prescription?

Yes, modafinil is still prescribed — but narrowly. Since a 2011 MHRA review, NHS prescribers only prescribe modafinil for narcolepsy, usually via a sleep clinic. For sleep apnoea or shift work sleep disorder, a GP will normally suggest other treatment first. A private prescriber can prescribe modafinil for a wider range of sleep disorders, but you'll pay for a consultation on top of the medicine.

Possible Side Effects, Hormonal Contraceptives and the Patient Information Leaflet

The most common side effects of modafinil are headache, nausea, dry mouth, reduced appetite and difficulty sleeping if you take modafinil late in the day. Most are mild and ease with regular use; the risk of side effects is lower at a 100mg dose. Serious adverse effects — a severe skin rash, chest pain, an allergic reaction — are rare but need immediate medical attention.

Modafinil interacts with hormonal contraceptives: it can make the pill and other hormonal birth control less effective, so use an additional method while taking it and for two months after stopping. Consult your doctor or pharmacist before use if you have a cardiovascular condition, a history of anxiety or depression, or if you are pregnant or breastfeed.
