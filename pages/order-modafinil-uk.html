What Is Modafinil and What Conditions Does It Treat?

Modafinil is a prescription medication that promotes wakefulness. In the UK it is licensed as a treatment for excessive sleepiness associated with narcolepsy, and doctors prescribe it off-label for other medical conditions including obstructive sleep apnoea and shift work sleep disorder. A doctor will normally diagnose the underlying condition before prescribing it, and NHS treatment is restricted to narcolepsy patients. It works on wakefulness pathways in the brain to improve alertness and reduce daytime sleepiness without the jittery, irregular effect of traditional stimulants.

Outside those conditions, many people use modafinil to stay awake and focused during long working days, to study, or to manage chronic fatigue. Clinical evidence suggests it improves attention and memory most reliably in people who are sleep-deprived.

Modafinil Dosage and Advice

The usual dose is one 200mg tablet taken in the morning. If you're new to modafinil, split a tablet and start with the equivalent of 100mg tablets — many users find that's enough, and side effects are milder. Don't take it after midday, or you'll struggle to sleep. Avoid combining modafinil with alcohol, which can reduce its effectiveness and increase side effects. Effects vary between people, so it's worth finding the lowest dose that works for you before using it regularly.

Side Effects, Patient Advice and Who Should Not Take Modafinil

Most patients taking modafinil have no problems. The most common side effects are headache, nausea, dry mouth, reduced appetite and mild anxiety, and they usually ease with regular use. Anxiety and sleep disruption are more likely at higher doses, so a lower dose is the first thing to try if either bothers you. Serious reactions are rare: stop and seek immediate medical attention if you develop a severe skin reaction, chest pain, difficulty breathing, an irregular heartbeat or swelling of the face or throat.

Modafinil is not suitable for every patient. Ask a doctor or pharmacy for medical advice before ordering if you have a history of heart problems, an anxiety disorder, depression or another serious medical condition, or if you are pregnant or breastfeeding. Inform your doctor of any medication you take — modafinil can reduce the effectiveness of hormonal contraception, and combining it with other treatments should be checked by a healthcare professional. If you're already receiving treatment for a sleep condition, don't stop prescribed medication to switch to modafinil without advice. Full details are on our modafinil side effects page.
