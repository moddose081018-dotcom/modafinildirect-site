What Are the Side Effects of Taking Modafinil?

The side effects of taking modafinil are, for most people, mild and temporary. Headache, nausea, reduced appetite, trouble sleeping — the common ones are usually tied to dosage and tend to diminish with regular use. A small number of people experience more significant reactions and should stop immediately.

Medical disclaimer: This page is for general information only. It does not replace the advice of a qualified healthcare professional. If you experience severe side effects, stop taking modafinil and seek medical attention.

Common Side Effects

These occur in a meaningful number of users and are generally mild:

  • Headache — the most frequently reported side effect. Usually mild. Often linked to dehydration; drinking more water helps.
  • Nausea — more common when taken on an empty stomach. Taking modafinil with a small amount of food typically reduces this.
  • Insomnia or difficulty sleeping — modafinil promotes wakefulness for 8–12 hours after taking it. Taking it too late in the day is the most common cause of sleep disruption.
  • Reduced appetite — many users report less interest in food during the active period. This is generally not harmful for healthy adults but warrants attention if you're already eating below maintenance.
  • Dry mouth — common; easily managed by drinking water throughout the day.
  • Anxiety or jitteriness — less common, and more likely at higher doses or in people sensitive to stimulants.

Less Common Side Effects

These occur in fewer users and are generally not serious:

  • Dizziness
  • Back pain
  • Diarrhoea
  • Rhinitis (runny nose)
  • Elevated heart rate
  • Increased blood pressure (relevant for people with existing cardiovascular conditions)

Serious Side Effects — When to Stop Immediately

A small number of people experience serious adverse reactions. Stop taking modafinil and seek medical attention if you experience:

  • Skin rash, blistering or peeling — can indicate Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, a rare but serious condition. This requires immediate medical attention.
  • Chest pain or irregular heartbeat
  • Severe allergic reaction — swelling of the face, lips, tongue or throat; difficulty breathing
  • Hallucinations or psychosis
  • Severe mood changes or suicidal thoughts

These reactions are rare, but they're the reason modafinil is a prescription medicine. If you have a history of heart problems, psychiatric conditions, or skin hypersensitivity, speak to a doctor before use.

How to Minimise Side Effects

The most common side effects are avoidable with straightforward precautions:

  • Take it early. Most people take modafinil first thing in the morning. Taking it after midday increases the risk of sleep disruption later.
  • Drink water. Modafinil is dehydrating. A significant number of headaches are simply dehydration headaches. Keep water nearby throughout the day.
  • Eat something first. Not a full meal necessarily — but taking modafinil on a completely empty stomach increases nausea for some people.
  • Start with a lower dose. Many people find that half a tablet (100mg) is effective with fewer side effects than a full 200mg dose. It's worth finding your minimum effective dose before going full strength.
  • Don't take it every day. Using modafinil 2–3 times per week rather than daily is widely reported to reduce tolerance build-up and side effect accumulation.
