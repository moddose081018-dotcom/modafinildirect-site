What Is Modafinil Used For?

Modafinil is a wakefulness-promoting medicine originally developed as a treatment for narcolepsy — a chronic sleep disorder that causes excessive daytime sleepiness. It's also used for sleepiness disorders such as obstructive sleep apnoea and shift work sleep disorder. It works on dopamine and other wakefulness pathways in the brain, improving alertness and concentration without the crash of traditional stimulants.

Because of that, modafinil is widely used off-label by students, university researchers and professionals who want better focus and motivation on long days. Clinical evidence suggests it is most effective for adults who are sleep-deprived, and that its benefit for well-rested people is more modest.

Side Effects and Health Advice

Most users tolerate modafinil well. The most common side effects are headache, nausea, dry mouth, reduced appetite and difficulty sleeping if taken too late in the day. Avoid taking it after midday, drink plenty of water and start with half a tablet if you're new to it.

Serious reactions are rare but you should stop taking it and seek medical help if you develop a skin rash, chest pain or an allergic reaction. If you have a diagnosed heart condition, a history of anxiety or depression, or you're pregnant or breastfeeding, get professional healthcare advice before use.
