What Is Modafinil Used For?

Modafinil is a prescription medication used to treat excessive daytime sleepiness. In the UK it is licensed for people with narcolepsy, and doctors also prescribe it for other sleep disorders such as obstructive sleep apnoea and shift work sleep disorder. It works on dopamine and other pathways in the brain to promote wakefulness and alertness, without the jittery, irregular energy of a traditional stimulant. That's why it's often called the "smart drug" — students, shift workers and professionals use it to stay alert and productive on long days.

Can a normal person take modafinil? Physically, yes — it's generally considered safe and effective in healthy adults, and it's not a controlled substance in the UK. But it is a prescription-only medicine, and a doctor would usually diagnose a sleep disorder before prescribing it. If you have any medical history that gives you pause, get medical advice from a healthcare professional first.

Side Effects

Most people who get modafinil delivered from us take it without any problems. The common side effects are headache, nausea, dry mouth, reduced appetite and trouble sleeping if taken too late. Long-term use can lead to tolerance, and a small number of people experience adverse effects such as anxiety or a rapid heartbeat. Serious reactions — a severe skin rash, an allergy with swelling or breathing difficulty, chest pain — are rare but need immediate medical attention.

Don't take modafinil if you're pregnant or breastfeed, if you have a history of heart valve problems, or if you're allergic to modafinil or lactose (an excipient in most generics). It can reduce the effectiveness of hormonal contraception. Full details, including how to minimise side effects, are on our modafinil side effects page.
